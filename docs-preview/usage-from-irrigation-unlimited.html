    Coming from Irrigation Unlimited¶

    Irrigation Unlimited (IU) is a scheduler and a sequencer of valves with no water model: it does not know the weather, the evapotranspiration or how much water a zone has lost. Smart Irrigation does, and until now the two were joined by two automations written by hand (adjust_time into IU, reset_bucket at the end).

    The full controller does the scheduling and the running itself. This page says where each thing you used in IU went. It can also bring your configuration over for you: see Importing your configuration.

    The words¶

    In Irrigation Unlimited In Smart Irrigation
    Controller (and its master entity) Supply: a pump or a main valve, with a delay before and after that can be negative
    Zone Zone, with its linked valve (and, if it needs several, other valves)
    Sequence Program
    Sequence zone (a step) Step: one zone, or several watered together
    Step with zones in parallel A step with several zones, watered at the same time
    Delay between steps, including a negative one Wait after this step; a negative value overlaps the steps (up to an hour)
    adjust_time (actual, percentage, increase, decrease) The step's duration: calculated (the default), calculated times a percentage, or fixed, with a minimum, a maximum (min_seconds, max_seconds) and an offset in seconds (adjust_seconds: -120, YAML only, see YAML-only options). For a temporary change of a whole program, smart_irrigation.adjust_program (a percent and/or seconds, for some hours or until reset). You no longer need an automation to hand it the duration, because the calculated one is the default.
    reset_bucket or set_bucket after the run Nothing to do: a run of ours credits the water balance itself, for the water that went through
    suspend smart_irrigation.suspend, for a zone or a program, for some hours or until a date
    manual_run smart_irrigation.water_zone or smart_irrigation.run_program. A manual run joins the queue and takes its turn by default (mode: queue); mode: replace stops what is running first. run_program also takes seconds, a total shared between the steps, and stop_program ends one program.
    cancel smart_irrigation.stop_watering
    Pause and resume smart_irrigation.pause_watering and resume_watering
    Skip to the next step smart_irrigation.next_step
    Schedule (time, sun, cron) A program's schedules: time of day or sun (with an offset, and a fallback time for polar days, fallback_time: previous, YAML only, see YAML-only options), with the days as filters. Cron is not supported as such; the import turns a cron line with a bounded set of times into time schedules.
    anchor: start and anchor: finish The schedule's moment is when the program starts, or when it must be done (the start is worked back from the length of the run)
    Weekdays, day of the month, even/odd, every_n_days, months, from/to The same filters, on each schedule: days of the week, every N days, and a period of the year that may span New Year, in the form; the shift of every N days (every_offset: 1), even or odd days (parity: even), named days of the month (days_of_month: [1, 15, last]) and months (months: [5, 6, 7, 8, 9]) are YAML only, see YAML-only options
    entity_states Not the same feature. In IU it lets a timed valve receive only the on command, only the off, or neither. We have no such switch. The nearest things are the per-zone safety off mode (auto, zha, off), which can arm a hardware timed off (MQTT topic, or a ZHA command) as a dead-man behind the normal close, and the check while a valve is open. The ZHA timed off is armed automatically for a Sonoff SWV (tested on a real valve, ZHA, firmware 1.0.04: it honours the command and counts the time in seconds); for any other ZHA model it is opt-in (zha mode) because the unit of the time is unverified. A Sonoff SWV in auto mode is now also opened by that timed command, so it never runs without its own timer. See closed loop.
    check_back (delay, retries) The zone's Wait for the valve to confirm and Send the open again (open_resends, YAML only: the zone's Edit in YAML); the wait is in its advanced settings. The wait applies to the open and the close. A valve that closes by itself while it should be open is caught by the check every 60 seconds and is not reopened (see sync_error below)
    irrigation_unlimited_start and finish events smart_irrigation_program_started and program_finished
    valve_on and valve_off events smart_irrigation_valve_on and valve_off, for zones and supplies
    sync_error smart_irrigation_valve_out_of_sync, with a smart_irrigation_zone_problem of reason valve_closed_early: a valve held open that reads closed
    switch_error smart_irrigation_zone_problem (a zone valve) and supply_problem (a pump or main valve)

    What is different¶

    • Programs never overlap. IU lets overlapping runs go on together; here a second program waits for the first, and a step with several zones (or a negative wait between steps) is how you ask for zones at the same time. A pump on a home network seldom has the pressure for more.
    • The weather is part of the schedule. A schedule skips, shortens or holds back what the skip conditions say. Turn it off per schedule if you want a plain timer.
    • The default duration is the one Smart Irrigation calculated, not a number you type.
    • Nothing is configured in YAML. It is all in the panel, and a restart goes on with the steps still to do instead of dropping a run that was under way.

    Importing your configuration¶

    In Settings, General, the card Import from Irrigation Unlimited opens a wizard that reads your IU configuration and turns it into zones, programs, their schedules and supplies. It takes the configuration from where IU has it: your YAML (configuration.yaml and its includes, as Home Assistant loaded it), IU's own settings when it was set up in the interface, or YAML you paste. Pasted YAML is read as text: !include, !secret and !env_var are not followed, and the report says so.

    Nothing is changed before you have seen it all:

    1. Zones: each IU zone is matched to one of your zones (by the one you choose, then by its valve, the blueprint that feeds it, or its name) or created. A zone you already have keeps its settings and only gets the fields it had empty.
    2. Programs and supplies: what each IU sequence and controller becomes, step by step. A sequence fed by Smart Irrigation's durations (an adjust-time automation) waters the calculated duration; the others keep IU's fixed times. The automation may adjust the sequence (with its zones) or each zone's own entity (binary_sensor.irrigation_unlimited_c1_z1, …): IU applies a zone's adjustment inside every sequence that waters it, so a step whose zones are all fed that way is calculated too.
    3. The report: what was carried over exactly, what was approximated (a cron expression turned into times, for example) and what has no equivalent here.

    The schedules of new programs are created switched off, so IU keeps watering until you are ready to switch over. The wizard says so differently when IU is not running, for instance because Home Assistant rejected its configuration: nothing waters then until you switch the schedules on.

    Switching the full controller on, which the programs need, creates a program with every zone that has a valve when there is no program yet. If you import first, the imported programs are the ones that water: a program only waters the zones of its steps. A program of your own that waters the same zones as the imported ones waters them twice: switch its schedules off under Programs. Importing again later updates what the earlier import made, in place, and removes what IU no longer has; the wizard asks before it removes anything. Only an administrator can import.

    Switching over¶

    At the top of the import page, once something is imported:

    • Switch over to Smart Irrigation switches off IU's controllers that are on (irrigation_unlimited.disable, which IU keeps across a restart), then switches on the imported schedules that IU had on. A sequence or zone schedule that IU runs switched off, or whose controller is off, stays off here too. A zone that IU runs switched off inside a sequence is named on the page: IU leaves it out, but here its step waters it until you switch that step off. If the import has no record of which schedules IU had on (an import from before this was kept), none of that program's schedules comes on and the page says so: switch them on yourself, or go back, import again and switch over again. IU is switched off first, so for a moment nothing waters rather than both.
    • Go back to Irrigation Unlimited switches the imported schedules off first, then on again the controllers the switch turned off, and no others. Which schedules were on is kept: switching over again brings back those, including your own changes.
    • If a step fails, the one before it is undone, and the page says who waters.
    • Who waters is read from what is on, not from the buttons: if IU switches a controller on again (by hand, or because IU does not restore its state) while imported schedules are on, or you switch a schedule on by hand while IU waters, the page says that both would water and offers to switch IU off. It also says when nothing waters.
    • What still uses Irrigation Unlimited lists your automations and scripts that name IU's services, events (irrigation_unlimited_start, _finish, …) or entities, with a link to each. An adjust-time automation has nothing left to do: the steps are calculated here. Nothing is changed for you.

    After the switch, importing again keeps the on or off you gave each schedule, and a schedule new to the import comes with IU's own on and off, since IU no longer waters.

    What the import does not carry over¶

    • A controller with a master becomes a supply, and its programs water beside the other controllers', as in IU. Controllers without a master share the mains: their programs run one after the other.
    • Runs that IU overlapped run one after the other.
    • A step's repeat becomes passes: the water in parts with a soak between, not full runs.
    • entity_states, threshold, a zone's default duration for manual runs, the master's check_back and volume, an unbounded cron, a sun event other than sunrise and sunset.

    By hand¶

    If you would rather rebuild than import:

    1. Switch on the full controller.
    2. Add your pump or main valve as a supply, and pick it in each zone.
    3. Make a program per IU sequence: its steps are the sequence's zones, in order. Leave the duration on calculated.
    4. Add a schedule to each. If IU ran it to finish at sunrise, take Sunrise, must be done.
    5. Switch IU's own schedules off, and remove the two automations that handed it durations and reset the water balance.
    6. Look at the Planning card: it shows the next days (3, 7 or 14, as you pick on the chart). Compare it with what IU would have done before you remove IU.

    Not in Smart Irrigation¶

    • Cron schedules (the import expands a bounded one into time schedules).
    • Several independent controllers, each with its own sequences. Several supplies are possible, and each zone picks its own, but there is one program queue.
    • Overlap of whole programs: only the steps of one program can overlap (negative wait), never two programs.
    • An adjustable granularity, renaming the entities IU creates, a built-in simulator.
    • IU's entity_states choice of which commands a timed valve receives.

    Not in Irrigation Unlimited¶

    • A water model: evapotranspiration, a water balance per zone, and a water balance credit for the water that really went through.
    • Durations calculated from the weather, instead of typed.
    • Weather skip conditions (rain, frost, wind, wet soil) that can be set per schedule.
    • Hardware dead-man arming (MQTT or ZHA) and a check that a valve did not close by itself while it should be open.
